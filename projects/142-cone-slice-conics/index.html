Definition

A conic section is the locus of points P where e = PF / d is constant.
e = \frac{PF}{d} = 0.871 (Ellipse: 0 < e < 1)
Trace point P along curve
PF / d = 0.871
✦

Why it works

1 1. Slice
▼

A plane cuts both nappes of a cone at a fixed tilt angle α and height h.

e = \frac{\sin\alpha}{\sin\phi}
• α (Tilt angle): angle between cutting plane & base.
• φ (Semi-vertical angle): angle of cone generator.
• h (Height): position along axis.
2 2. Locus
▼

For any point P on the intersection, the ratio of focus distance PF to directrix distance d is constant:

e = \frac{PF}{d} = \text{constant}
Current ratio: PF / d = 0.871
3 3. Conic type
▼

The eccentricity e determines the curve:

Circle (e = 0): Horizontal plane (α = 0°).
Ellipse (0 < e < 1): Cuts one nappe (α < φ).
Parabola (e = 1): Parallel to generator (α = φ).
Hyperbola (e > 1): Cuts both nappes (α > φ).
* e = 0 is a Circle (special ellipse).
What happens at the borders?
▼

At the boundary angle α = φ (e = 1), the ellipse opens towards infinity, turning into a parabola. When α exceeds φ, the cutting plane strikes both nappes of the double cone, creating two mirrored hyperbolic branches!

Circle → Ellipse → Parabola → Hyperbola

2. Constant ratio
\frac{PF}{d} = e
PF (to focus) & d (to directrix) keep a fixed ratio as P moves.

3. Locus forms All points P form the conic section.
The Dandelin Spheres Theorem: When an inscribed sphere is placed inside the cone such that it is tangent to both the cone surface and the cutting plane, the point of contact with the plane becomes the geometric Focus (F)! The intersection of the sphere's contact circle with the cutting plane produces the Directrix line (ℓ). This proves why every slice is precisely a conic section.

Connection

The conic's shape is uniquely determined by e, which is set by α (and φ).

α ↑  &implies;  e ↑

As tilt angle α increases from 0° to 90°, the intersection transitions continuously from Circle → Ellipse → Parabola → Hyperbola.
